# 1 John 1–5: summary

**Coverage:** 5 chapters, 105 verses, the same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 12 flags.
- By category: TEXT 9, INTERP 3.
- By severity: 11 at sev 1, 1 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** Most TR differences in 1 John are small expansions:
  - "Christ" added (1:7, 4:3)
  - "him" added (4:19)
  - a repeated clause (5:13)
  - "and so we are" dropped (3:1)
- **The opposite direction (2:23).** The KJV prints the second half of 2:23 in italics, marking it as doubtful, because the Byzantine manuscripts lacked it. The early text includes it.

## Most important differences

1. **5:7–8 (sev 3).** The Johannine Comma: "the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost: and these three are one". It is found in no Greek manuscript before the late medieval period, in no early version and in no early Greek Father. It came from Latin commentary. It is the most famous TR reading with no claim to be original, and the only explicit Trinitarian formula in the KJV New Testament.
2. **3:9.** "he cannot sin". The present tense means "cannot keep on sinning". The KJV wording seems to contradict 1:8–10.
3. **2:20.** "ye know all things". The early text reads "you all have knowledge". John is rejecting a spiritual elite, not claiming that his readers are omniscient.
4. **5:18.** "keepeth himself". The early text has "keeps him": the Son of God keeps the believer.
5. **4:19.** "We love him". The early text has "We love". Love as such is the response to God's love.

## Assessment

The KJV's rendering of 1 John is simple and faithful, and its key sayings match a modern translation: "God is light", "God is love", "perfect love casteth out fear". Apart from one interpolation, the differences are slight. That interpolation, the Comma at 5:7, is the single most consequential textual difference in the KJV New Testament after Mark 16:9–20 and John 7:53–8:11. It is the only one of the three that was added to support a doctrine.

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses.*
