## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- LORD or GOD for YHWH. "LORD of hosts" for *YHWH tseba'ot*.
- "Groves" for the Asherim.
- "Hell" or "the grave" for Sheol.
- "Meat offering" for the grain offering (*minchah*).
- "Burden" for *massa'* (oracle) in the headings of chs. 13–23.
- "Strange" for "foreign".
- Animal names in the desolation oracles (13, 34, 35, 43) are rendered "dragons", "satyrs", "owls", "unicorns". Only one is flagged per passage.

## Most important differences

1. **7:14: "a virgin shall conceive"** against *ha'almah harah*, "the young woman is pregnant". The KJV follows the LXX *parthenos* and Matthew 1:23. INTERP 3.
2. **14:12: "O Lucifer, son of the morning"** against *helel ben-shachar*, "Shining One, son of Dawn". It is a taunt against the king of Babylon. The Latin name later fed the Satan tradition. NAME 3.
3. **9:2 (KJV 9:3): "and not increased the joy"** follows the ketiv. The qere gives "increased its joy". TEXT 2.
4. **52:15: "so shall he sprinkle many nations"** may be "startle". This is debated and theologically weighty for the Servant Song.
5. **40:3: "the voice of him that crieth in the wilderness"** follows the Gospels' LXX phrasing. The Hebrew accents read "in the wilderness prepare the way".
6. **59:19: "When the enemy shall come in like a flood, the Spirit of the LORD shall lift up a standard"** should be "he will come like a rushing stream which the wind of Yahweh drives". It is a popular verse with no basis in the Hebrew.
7. **28:10: "precept upon precept, line upon line"** is mocking gibberish (*tsav la-tsav*) in the Hebrew. The KJV made it a teaching principle.
8. **30:7: "their strength is to sit still"** is Egypt nicknamed "Rahab who sits still". **65:11: "that troop... that number"** are the gods Gad (Fortune) and Meni (Destiny).

## Assessment

The KJV Isaiah is a magnificent and largely faithful rendering. Its great passages, including chs. 6, 40, 53 and 55, track the Hebrew closely.

The distinctive divergences come from three sources:

1. **Christological readings drawn from the LXX and NT** at key cruxes: 7:14, 40:3, 52:15, 64:3.
2. **Ketiv/qere and vocalisation choices**, several now confirmed or corrected by the Dead Sea Isaiah scroll (1QIsa-a): 9:2, 19:18, 21:8, 45:2, 49:5, 49:12.
3. **Pre-modern lexicography** for rare words, especially animals, plants and objects.

About two dozen verses differ in a way a careful reader would care about. A handful of popular quotations rest on mistranslations: 28:10, 30:7, 59:19.
