# James 1–5: summary

**Coverage:** 5 chapters, 108 verses, the same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 13 flags.
- By category: TEXT 5, INTERP 3, LEXICAL 3, ARCHAIC 2.
- By severity: all 13 are sev 1.

## Systematic

- **"Temptation" for *peirasmos*.** James uses the word in two senses. In 1:2–12 it means a trial that produces endurance; in 1:13–14 it means enticement to sin. The KJV renders both "temptation", which loses the distinction.
- **Textual base.** The TR differs in five places:
  - "dead" for "useless" (2:20), which loses James's pun on *erga/arge*;
  - "God" for "Lord" (3:9);
  - "adulterers and adulteresses" for James's feminine image of unfaithful Israel (4:4);
  - "faults" for "sins" (5:16);
  - a longer wording in 3:12.

## Most important differences

1. **2:2.** "your assembly". The Greek has "synagogue". This is the only NT use of the word for a Christian gathering, and the KJV's rendering hides it.
2. **5:16.** "Confess your faults … effectual fervent prayer". The TR has "faults" where the early text has "sins". "Fervent" has no basis in the Greek.
3. **1:2.** "divers temptations". James means trials. The joy he urges is in being tested, not in being tempted.
4. **4:5.** An obscure verse. The two versions take different words as the subject.
5. **Archaic words.** "Superfluity of naughtiness" (1:21) and "the Lord is very pitiful" (5:11) mislead a modern reader.

## Assessment

James's plain moral Greek comes through well in the KJV, including the faith-and-works argument (2:14–26), which the two versions render alike. The flags are small. A cluster of archaic words now reads comically or misleadingly: "naughtiness", "pitiful", "masters" for teachers, "vile raiment".

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses.*
