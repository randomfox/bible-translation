# Mark 1–16: summary

- **Coverage:** 16 chapters and 673 SBLGNT verses. The KJV has 678. Five KJV verses have no counterpart in the critical text: 7:16, 9:44, 9:46, 11:26 and 15:28.
- **Flags:** 39 flags on 39 verses.
  - By category: TEXT 32, INTERP 5, LEXICAL 1, NAME 1.
  - By severity: 26 at sev 1, 12 at sev 2, 1 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** As in Matthew, most differences come from the Greek text each version follows. The KJV follows the Textus Receptus, which in Mark often:
  - harmonises with Matthew and Luke (2:17, 6:11, 10:21, 11:26, 13:14, 14:22),
  - repeats a phrase from elsewhere in Mark (7:16, 9:44, 9:46), and
  - adds pious expansions (9:24 "with tears", 9:29 "and fasting", 10:24 "them that trust in riches").
- **Recurring terms.** Not flagged per verse:
  - "Holy Ghost" for the Holy Spirit.
  - "devils" for *daimonia* (demons).
  - "hell" for *geenna* (Gehenna).
  - "straightway" and "immediately" for Mark's *euthys*. Both versions keep this word.

## Most important differences

1. **16:9–20 (sev 3).** The KJV prints the longer ending as Mark's own text, with no comment. It is absent from Sinaiticus and Vaticanus and differs from Mark in style. SBLGNT double-brackets it. The KJV also omits the shorter ending (16:8).
2. **1:1.** The KJV reads "Jesus Christ, the Son of God". SBLGNT reads only "Jesus Christ". The manuscripts are genuinely divided.
3. **1:41.** The KJV reads "moved with compassion". SBLGNT reads "moved with anger". The harder reading was adopted, and the choice is disputed.
4. **1:2.** The KJV reads "in the prophets". The earliest manuscripts read "in Isaiah the prophet". Scribes apparently corrected Mark, who credits a quotation of Malachi plus Isaiah to Isaiah alone.
5. **7:19.** The KJV reads "purging all meats", which makes the words part of a description of digestion. The best text reads "(Thus he declared all foods clean)", which is Mark's comment on the food laws.
6. **9:29.** The KJV reads "by prayer and fasting". The earliest text has "by prayer" only.
7. **10:24.** The KJV reads "for them that trust in riches". This addition softens a hard saying.
8. **7:16, 9:44, 9:46, 11:26, 15:28.** These are whole verses absent from the earliest manuscripts.

## Assessment

Mark's KJV is accurate in how it renders the Greek. Only seven of the 38 flags concern translation rather than the underlying text: 3:21 "friends", 4:11 "mystery", 7:19 and 8:36 "soul". The real divergence is textual. The Byzantine text that the KJV follows smooths Mark's abrupt style. It removes his "error" in 1:2 and adds familiar phrases from the other Gospels. Most significantly, it presents the second-century longer ending as authentic, so the KJV Mark ends with appearances and signs, not with the women's frightened silence at 16:8. Mark has the highest concentration of severity 2–3 textual flags of any Gospel so far.

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses.*
