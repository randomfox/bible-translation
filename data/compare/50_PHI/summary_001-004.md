# Philippians 1–4: summary

**Coverage:** 4 chapters, 104 verses, the same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 9 flags.
- By category: LEXICAL 4, MEANING 2, TEXT 2, INTERP 1.
- By severity: 7 at sev 1, 2 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Changed English.** Most flags come from words whose meaning has shifted since 1611:
  - "conversation" for citizenship (3:20).
  - "vile body" for the body of humiliation (3:21).
  - "moderation" for gentleness (4:5).
  - "bishops" for local overseers (1:1).
  - "palace" for the praetorian guard (1:13).
- **Textual base.** There are few TR differences: an added rule-clause at 3:16, and "Christ" at 4:13.

## Most important differences

1. **2:6 (sev 2).** "thought it not robbery to be equal with God". The KJV makes Christ claim equality with God as rightfully his. Paul's point is the reverse: Christ did not treat equality with God as something to be exploited, but emptied himself.
2. **2:7 (sev 2).** "made himself of no reputation" for *heauton ekenosen*, "emptied himself". This weakens the key verb of the Christ hymn. The word is the source of the theological term *kenosis*.
3. **4:13.** "I can do all things through Christ". The verse is about contentment in plenty and in want (4:11–12). It is not a promise of unlimited ability. The TR also adds "Christ".
4. **3:20.** "our conversation is in heaven". The Philippians lived in a Roman colony, and Paul tells them their true citizenship is in heaven. The 1611 word now hides this.

## Assessment

Philippians is a short, warm letter, and the KJV gives a faithful version of it. The one substantive weakness is in the Christ hymn (2:6–11), the theological centre of the letter. There the KJV's "not robbery" and "of no reputation" turn Paul's picture of self-emptying into one of a rightful claim and a mere loss of status. Everything else either matches a modern translation or uses English whose meaning has since shifted.

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses.*
