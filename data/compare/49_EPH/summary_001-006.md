# Ephesians 1–6: summary

**Coverage:** 6 chapters, 155 verses. Same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 16 flags.
- By category: TEXT 11, INTERP 2, MEANING 1, LEXICAL 1, ARCHAIC 1.
- By severity: all 16 at sev 1.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The TR adds short phrases taken from Paul's other letters:
  - "of our Lord Jesus Christ" (3:14)
  - "by Jesus Christ" (3:9)
  - "of his flesh, and of his bones" (5:30, from Gen 2:23)
  - "fruit of the Spirit" for "fruit of light" (5:9, from Gal 5:22)
- **The address.** The early text may not name Ephesus at all (1:1). The words are absent from P46, Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, which suggests a circular letter. The KJV presents it as a letter to one city.

## Most important differences

1. **4:12.** KJV "for the perfecting of the saints, for the work of the ministry". The so-called "fatal comma" makes ministry the clergy's job. Paul says leaders equip the saints *for* the work of ministry.
2. **5:21–22.** KJV "in the fear of God … Wives, submit yourselves". In the early text, "submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ" (5:21) supplies the verb for 5:22. Mutual submission therefore frames the instructions to wives. In the KJV they read as a new, separate command.
3. **3:15.** KJV "the whole family in heaven and earth". Paul says *every* family takes its name from the Father, a pun on *pater/patria*.
4. **3:9.** KJV "fellowship of the mystery" for *oikonomia*, the plan or administration.
5. **6:12.** KJV "spiritual wickedness in high places" for "spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places". This is the same phrase rendered "heavenly places" in 1:3, 1:20 and 2:6.
6. **4:9.** KJV "descended first into the lower parts of the earth". It has often been read as a descent into hell. The phrase is more likely the incarnation.

## Assessment

The KJV renders Ephesians' long, liturgical sentences well. Its great passages are faithful to the Greek: 2:8–10, 3:14–21, 4:4–6 and the armour of God in 6:10–17. No flag is severe. Even so, two small points of punctuation and textual base, at 4:12 and 5:21–22, have shaped church practice more than their size suggests: one bears on the role of clergy, the other on the household code.
