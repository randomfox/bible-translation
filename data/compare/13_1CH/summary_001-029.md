# 1 Chronicles 1–29: summary

**Coverage:** 29 chapters and 943 verses, counted by Hebrew numbering. Hebrew 5:27–41 corresponds to KJV 6:1–15, so chapters 5–6 are numbered differently in the two versions.

**Flags:** 43 flags.
- By category: INTERP 16, TEXT 7, LEXICAL 6, NAME 6, ARCHAIC 5, MEANING 3.
- By severity: 39 at sev 1, 4 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Genealogies (chapters 1–9).** Most of the NAME and TEXT flags fall here. Names are spelled differently in the two versions, and the Chronicler sometimes departs from the parallel lists in Samuel–Kings.
- **Parallels with Samuel–Kings.** Chronicles reuses much of 2 Samuel. The KJV renders the shared passages consistently with its Samuel, including the harsh reading of the Ammonite war (20:3).

## Most important differences

1. **21:1.** "And Satan stood up against Israel". The Hebrew *satan* has no article, unlike *ha-satan* in Job and Zechariah. It may mean "an adversary". The verse rewrites 2 Sam 24:1, where God himself incites David. Read with the article, it becomes a key text for a personal Satan.
2. **20:3.** "cut them with saws". This follows a variant reading. In the parallel at 2 Sam 12:31 the captives are set to labour with saws, not executed with them.
3. **17:17.** One of the most obscure phrases in Chronicles. Neither version is certain.
4. **28:12.** "by the spirit". The Hebrew *ruah* can mean David's own mind or the divine Spirit. The KJV leaves "spirit" lowercase.

## Assessment

The KJV's 1 Chronicles is accurate on the whole. The largest interpretive question is "Satan" at 21:1. It matters for the history of Jewish and Christian ideas about the devil, but the text is genuinely ambiguous.
