# Revelation 18–22

**Scope:** 5 chapters, 108 verses. 33 verses flagged with 37 differences in total.

- By category: TEXT 26, ARCHAIC 5, MEANING 3, INTERP 3
- By severity: 1 (nuance) 28, 2 (notable) 7, 3 (major) 2

## Systematic differences

- **Textual base.** Most differences come from the Greek text, not from translation choices. The KJV follows the TR, and in Revelation that rests on very few manuscripts, plus Erasmus's back-translation from the Latin in the last verses of ch. 22. SBLGNT follows the much broader manuscript evidence.
- **Recurring terms.**
  - The KJV has "devils" for *daimonia* (demons), "bottomless pit" for *abyssos*, "hell" for *hadēs*, "beasts" for the *zōa* (living creatures), "servants" for *douloi* (slaves), and "fornication" for *porneia*.
  - "Come quickly" (*tachy*) means "soon".

## Most important differences

1. **22:19.** KJV "out of the book of life"; mine "from the tree of life". Every Greek manuscript reads "tree". The TR's "book" comes from Erasmus's Latin-based back-translation.
2. **22:14.** KJV "they that do his commandments"; mine "those who wash their robes". The two Greek readings look alike in uncial script. SBLGNT's reading echoes 7:14 and places the blessing in being cleansed rather than in keeping the law.
3. **22:6.** KJV "God of the holy prophets"; mine "God of the spirits of the prophets". SBLGNT presents God as the one who inspires the prophets.
4. **21:24.** KJV "the nations of them which are saved"; mine "the nations". The TR adds a qualifier that limits who walks in the city's light.
5. **18:20.** KJV "ye holy apostles"; mine "you saints and apostles". The TR merges two of the three groups.
6. **18:3.** KJV "have drunk"; mine "have fallen". The two Greek verbs differ by a single letter.
7. **19:8.** KJV "the righteousness of saints"; mine "the righteous deeds of the saints". The plural *dikaiōmata* means acts.
8. **20:14.** KJV "death and hell were cast into the lake of fire"; mine "Death and Hades". With "hell" for *hadēs*, the KJV seems to throw hell into hell.

## Assessment

In these chapters the KJV translates its Greek text faithfully, often word for word. Almost all of its meaningful departures come from the TR's weak textual base in Revelation, not from mistranslation. Most of these are small additions or substitutions, such as "I John", "from God", "honour" and "pure". Two are significant: "book of life" (22:19) and "do his commandments" (22:14). The KJV's real translation errors are few and minor: tense in 19:3, "his people" for "peoples" in 21:3, and "righteousness" for "righteous deeds" in 19:8. Archaic terms like "hell" for Hades and "beasts" for the living creatures can mislead a modern reader. On the whole a reader of the KJV gets the vision of Babylon's fall and the new Jerusalem accurately. The exceptions are the textual points at the end of the book.
