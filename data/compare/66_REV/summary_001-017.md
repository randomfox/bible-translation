# Revelation 1–17: summary

**Coverage:** 17 chapters, 297 verses. SBLGNT ends chapter 12 with 12:18, which is KJV 13:1a; otherwise the verses match the KJV.

**Flags:** 33 flags.
- By category: TEXT 24, INTERP 4, LEXICAL 3, MEANING 1, ARCHAIC 1.
- By severity: 32 at sev 1, 1 at sev 2.

Together with summary_018-022 (37 differences, 2 at sev 3), this covers the whole book: 405 verses and 70 flags.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** Revelation has the weakest Greek base of any NT book in the TR. Erasmus had a single incomplete twelfth-century manuscript, and in places he filled gaps from the Latin. Later editors also introduced conjecture. Three-quarters of the flags are textual:
  - **Completed formulas.** "and art to come" is added at 11:17, where the omission is deliberate. "the beginning and the ending" is added at 1:8.
  - **Additions from elsewhere in the book.** Examples are 1:11, 5:14 and 14:5.
  - **Lost words.** Words were dropped by homoeoteleuton, a scribe's eye skipping between similar endings, at 8:7 and 14:1.
  - **Readings with no Greek manuscript support.** "King of saints" (15:3) comes from Erasmus's text. "and shalt be" (16:5) is Beza's conjecture.
- **Recurring terms.** These are not flagged each time: "beasts" for the living creatures (flagged at 4:6), "bottomless pit" for the abyss (9:1), and "hell" for Hades (1:18, 6:8).

## Most important differences

1. **16:5 (sev 2).** "which art, and wast, and shalt be". This is a conjecture by Theodore Beza (1582); every Greek manuscript has "the Holy One". The KJV here translates a modern editor's guess, not the text of any manuscript.
2. **1:5–6, 5:9–10.** "washed us … kings and priests … redeemed us". The early text reads "freed us … a kingdom, priests … ransomed people (them)".
3. **10:6.** "there should be time no longer". The Greek means "no more delay". The KJV wording gave rise to the idea, and the hymn line, that time itself will end.
4. **13:8.** "the Lamb slain from the foundation of the world". The phrase more likely modifies "written", as in 17:8.
5. **15:3.** "King of saints". This has almost no Greek support. The early text reads "King of the nations".
6. **8:13.** An angel replaces John's eagle.
7. **11:17.** The added "and art to come" undoes John's deliberate point that, at the seventh trumpet, God has come.

## Assessment

The KJV translators rendered Revelation's Greek faithfully, and its visions read with great power. But Revelation is the book where the KJV's Greek base matters most. The TR was printed from too few manuscripts, so several of its readings are found in no Greek manuscript at all: 16:5 here, and Erasmus's Latin back-translations in 22:16–21. A KJV reader meets a Revelation partly edited by sixteenth-century scholars.
