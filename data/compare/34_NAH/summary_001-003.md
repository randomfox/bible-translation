# Nahum 1–3: summary

- **Coverage:** 3 chapters, 47 verses, in Hebrew numbering. Heb 2:1 is KJV 1:15, and Heb 2:2–14 is KJV 2:1–13.
- **Flags:** 3 flags on 3 verses.
  - By category: NAME 2, LEXICAL 1.
  - By severity: all three are severity 1.

## Systematic

- **Divine names:** YHWH appears as "LORD" and *YHWH tseva'ot* as "the LORD of hosts".
- **1:1:** the KJV reads "The burden of Nineveh". *Massa* means "oracle" here, as it does in the other prophets.
- **Textual base:** the Masoretic Text. Chapter 1 contains a partial acrostic (alef to kaf). Neither version shows it.

## Differences

1. **3:8**
   - KJV: "populous No".
   - Mine: "Thebes".
   - *No-Amon* is "No of Amon", the Egyptian Thebes. The verse compares Nineveh's coming fall to Thebes, which Assyria had sacked in 663 BC. The KJV's "populous" obscures that comparison.
2. **2:8** (KJV 2:7)
   - KJV: "Huzzab shall be led away captive".
   - Mine: "it is decreed".
   - The KJV turned a verb into a name, creating a queen called Huzzab.
3. **2:4** (KJV 2:3)
   - KJV: "the fir trees shall be terribly shaken".
   - Mine: "the cypress spears are brandished".

## Assessment

Nahum's vivid war poetry is hard Hebrew. The KJV renders it vigorously and, for the most part, accurately. The flags come from misidentified proper names and one image of spears taken as trees. None affects the message of Nineveh's fall. The text of 2:2–11 is uncertain in places, so all translations of those verses, mine included, are partly conjecture.

*Caveat: the KJV was checked at selected verses only.*
