# 1 Timothy 1–6: summary

**Coverage:** 6 chapters, 113 verses. Same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 15 flags.
- By category: TEXT 7, LEXICAL 3, MEANING 2, INTERP 2, ARCHAIC 1.
- By severity: 14 at sev 1, 1 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The TR adds small phrases borrowed from other Pauline letters:
  - "wise" (1:17)
  - "in Christ" (2:7)
  - "not greedy of filthy lucre" (3:3)
  - "in spirit" (4:12)
  - "from such withdraw thyself" (6:5)
- **Church offices.** The KJV renders *episkopos* "bishop" (3:1–2). Its italic "their wives" in 3:11 decides that the women named there are deacons' wives. The Greek leaves open that they could be women deacons.

## Most important differences

1. **3:16 (sev 2).** "God was manifest in the flesh". In uncial script, "God" (ΘΣ) and "who" (ΟΣ) differ by a single stroke. The earliest manuscripts read "He who was manifested", the opening of a hymn about Christ. The KJV reading made this a classic proof-text for the incarnation.
2. **6:10.** "the root of all evil". Paul says "a root of all kinds of evils", a weaker and more precise claim.
3. **6:5.** "supposing that gain is godliness". Subject and predicate are reversed. The error Paul condemns is treating godliness as a source of profit.
4. **6:20.** "science falsely so called". This means *gnosis*, "knowledge". It has nothing to do with natural science.
5. **2:12.** "usurp authority". This renders *authentein*, a rare and debated word. The KJV's choice assumes the authority would be illegitimate.
6. **5:12.** "damnation" for *krima*, here the censure a widow incurs by breaking her pledge.

## Assessment

The KJV renders the Pastoral instructions accurately. Two of its readings are often misquoted: "the love of money is the root of all evil", and the "science" of 6:20. The one doctrinally weighty textual point is 3:16. There the TR's "God" rests on a later reading. It is the clearest case in Paul's letters of a TR variant that strengthens an explicit statement of Christ's deity.
