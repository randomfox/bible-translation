# Hosea 1–14 — summary

**Coverage:** 14 chapters and 197 verses, in Hebrew numbering. Heb 2:1–2 = KJV 1:10–11, Heb 12:1 = KJV 11:12, and Heb 14:1 = KJV 13:16.

**Flags:** 17 flags on 17 verses.

- By category: INTERP 9, LEXICAL 3, NAME 3, MEANING 2.
- By severity: sev 1 for 16 flags, sev 2 for 1, sev 3 for none.

## Systematic

- **Divine names and recurring terms**
  - YHWH is rendered LORD throughout the KJV.
  - *hesed* ("steadfast love") is rendered "mercy"/"goodness" (6:4, 6:6).
  - "Sheol" becomes "the grave" in 13:14.
- **The hardest Hebrew text in the prophets.** In many lines, both versions guess at a corrupt or very compressed original: 4:18–19, 5:2, 7:4–6, 8:10, 10:9–10, 11:7, 12:1. I kept the Masoretic Text (MT) where possible. Three of my first-draft renderings followed the LXX or an emendation, and I corrected them back to the MT: 6:5, 9:13, 14:3/6.
- **Names and wordplay.** The KJV transliterates names and so hides the point of the wordplay: Lo-ruhamah, Lo-ammi, Ishi/Baali, and "king Jareb".

## Most important differences

1. **13:14**
   - KJV: "O death, I will be thy plagues".
   - Mine: "O Death, where are your plagues?"
   - *ehi* is "where?". Paul quotes the verse this way in 1 Cor 15:55, and the KJV's own NT has "where".
2. **13:10**
   - KJV: "I will be thy king".
   - Mine: "Where now is your king?"
   - This is the same word, *ehi*.
3. **5:13, 10:6**
   - KJV: "king Jareb".
   - Mine: "the great king".
   - The phrase is the Assyrian royal title; no king called Jareb is known.
4. **10:1**
   - KJV: "an empty vine".
   - Mine: "a luxuriant vine".
   - The rest of the verse speaks of abundant fruit.
5. **2:18** (KJV 2:16)
   - KJV: "Ishi … Baali".
   - The KJV transliterates a husband/Baal pun that the reader cannot follow.
6. **6:7**
   - KJV: "like men".
   - Mine: "like Adam".
   - The following "there" may point to the town of Adam.
7. **3:1**
   - KJV: "flagons of wine".
   - Mine: "cakes of raisins".
   - These were cultic raisin cakes.
8. **13:2**
   - KJV: "Let the men that sacrifice kiss the calves".
   - Mine: "Those who sacrifice human beings kiss calves!"

## Assessment

The KJV of Hosea is a careful rendering of a very difficult Hebrew text. Most of its divergences are defensible choices where the original is obscure (INTERP, sev 1). The only clear mistranslation is the *ehi* = "I will be" reading in 13:10 and 13:14, which makes the climactic taunt of Death into a promise. The KJV New Testament itself quotes the verse as a question. Readers lose most from the transliterated name puns. For this book, the bigger caveat is not the KJV but the Hebrew: several verses cannot be translated with confidence by anyone.

*Caveat: the KJV was checked at selected verses rather than read through in full.*
