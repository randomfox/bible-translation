## Systematic differences

- **Divine names:** KJV "the LORD" / "the Lord GOD" / "the LORD of hosts" for YHWH, Adonai YHWH, YHWH Tseva'ot; rendered here Yahweh, the Lord Yahweh, Yahweh of Armies (not flagged per verse).
- **massa'** ("burden", 9:1; 12:1) is a heading meaning "oracle".
- **Textual base:** the KJV follows the Masoretic Text closely; its only textual divergence in this section is a qere/ketiv choice (11:2). Difficult passages (13:5; 14:5–6) are handled by guesswork in both renderings.

## Most important differences

1. **13:6** — KJV "wounds in thine hands" vs "wounds on your chest": *beyn yadeykha* ("between your hands") is the torso; the context is the self-inflicted cuts of a discredited prophet, not hand wounds.
2. **9:9** — "having salvation" vs "victorious": *nosha'* is passive ("delivered, given victory"); the KJV makes the king the giver of salvation.
3. **11:17** — "the idol shepherd" vs "my worthless shepherd": *'elil* here means "worthless".
4. **11:7** — KJV "I will feed... even you, O poor of the flock" turns a past narrative into a future promise with a vocative.
5. **10:4** — "every oppressor" vs "every ruler": *noges* in a list of positive leadership images.
6. **12:2** — KJV makes Judah a second target of the siege; the Hebrew more likely says Judah is caught up in the siege of Jerusalem.
7. **14:21** — "the Canaanite" vs "a trader": *kena'ani* is commonly "merchant", fitting a verse about Temple vessels.
8. **9:15 / 9:16** — "subdue with sling stones" (they trample the enemy's sling stones) and "lifted up as an ensign" (*mitnosesot* "sparkle", of crown jewels).

## Assessment

In these late chapters of Zechariah the KJV is faithful in substance: the messianic passages (9:9–10; 11:12–13; 12:10; 13:7) are rendered accurately and from the Hebrew, including the MT's "look on me whom they have pierced". Most differences are lexical: rare words (*chazizim*, *ge'on hayyarden*, *mitnosesot*, *mamzer*) that the 1611 translators guessed at, and a handful of grammatical misreadings of tense or syntax. Only 13:6 noticeably changes what a reader would picture. The obscure verses (13:5; 14:6) remain uncertain in any translation.
