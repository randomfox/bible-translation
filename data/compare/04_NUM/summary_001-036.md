# Numbers 1–36: summary

**Coverage:** 36 chapters, 1,289 verses.

**Flags:** 96 flags.
- By category: INTERP 31, LEXICAL 25, ARCHAIC 22, MEANING 11, NAME 7.
- By severity: 81 at sev 1, 15 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Uncertain words.** Animal and material terms that were uncertain in 1611 follow the Septuagint or later tradition: "unicorn" for *re'em*, the wild ox (23:22, 24:8); "badgers' skins" (4:6); "giants" for the Nephilim (13:33).
- **"Leper" for *tsarua*** (5:2), as in Leviticus.
- **Words whose English sense has changed:** "stranger" for any unauthorised outsider (3:10), "suburbs" for pastureland (35:2), "apparently" in its 1611 sense of "openly" (12:8).

## Most important differences

1. **23:22, 24:8.** "the strength of an unicorn". Balaam's oracles compare Israel to the wild ox. The KJV's mythical beast comes from the Greek *monokeros*.
2. **11:25.** "they prophesied, and did not cease". The Hebrew says they did not do so again. The elders' prophesying was a one-time sign.
3. **14:34.** "my breach of promise". The Hebrew means God's opposition. The KJV wording suggests God broke his word.
4. **13:33.** "giants". The spies' report echoes the Nephilim of Gen 6:4.
5. **21:14.** "What he did in the Red sea". This is an obscure fragment of the "Book of the Wars of Yahweh"; it is now usually read as place names (Waheb in Suphah).

## Assessment

The narratives and law of Numbers are rendered faithfully in the KJV. The flags are mostly uncertain words and archaisms. The most visible legacy is the "unicorn" of Balaam's oracles. Together with Deut 33:17 and Job 39, it put a mythical animal into the English Bible. The census lists and itineraries are rendered alike in both versions.
