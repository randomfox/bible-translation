## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- LORD for YHWH.
- "Strange woman / stranger" for *'ishshah zarah / nokriyyah*, the adulteress or outsider woman. This is flagged once, at 2:16.
- "Froward", "naughty" and "subtil" are archaic moral terms.
- "Hell" or "the grave" for Sheol.
- "Rubies" for *peninim*, which is probably coral or pearls.
- The KJV routinely supplies verbs and comparatives in italics to fill the terse two-line sayings. Usually this is harmless.

## Most important differences

1. **29:18: "Where there is no vision, the people perish."** *Chazon* means prophetic revelation and *yippara'* means "run wild". The verse is about Torah and revelation, not leadership vision.
2. **16:1:** the KJV makes both the heart's plans and the tongue's answer come "from the LORD". The Hebrew contrasts "to man belong the plans" with "from Yahweh the answer".
3. **19:18: "let not thy soul spare for his crying"** should be "do not set your heart on his death". The KJV turns a limit on discipline into "ignore his cries".
4. **18:24: "must shew himself friendly"** should be "a man of many companions may come to ruin". The KJV took the wrong root.
5. **18:1:** the KJV praises the loner who "intermeddleth with all wisdom". The Hebrew censures him, because he "breaks out against all sound judgment".
6. **23:7: "as he thinketh in his heart, so is he"** describes a stingy host counting the cost of each bite. It is not a general maxim about thought.
7. **25:23: "The north wind driveth away rain"** should be "brings forth rain". The KJV reverses the image and the second line.
8. **22:20: "excellent things"** may be "thirty sayings", the reading that links 22:17–24:22 to the Egyptian Instruction of Amenemope. This is a text and vocalisation issue.

## Assessment

Proverbs has more substantive divergences per verse than narrative books. The reasons are its terse, often elliptical couplets and its many rare words: *mitlahamim*, *'amon*, *manon*, *'alqum*, *chetheph*.

Most KJV choices are defensible guesses that later lexicography (Ugaritic, Arabic, Egyptian parallels) has refined: spider/lizard, greyhound/rooster, conies/hyraxes.

About a dozen verses differ in a way an ordinary reader would care about. Several are popular quotations that have taken on meanings the Hebrew does not carry: 29:18, 23:7, 18:24, 11:30.

The ethical and theological content of the book is conveyed faithfully overall.
