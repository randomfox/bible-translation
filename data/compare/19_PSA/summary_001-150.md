## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- LORD for YHWH, and GOD for YHWH after Adonai.
- "mercy" or "lovingkindness" for *hesed*.
- Sheol is rendered "hell" or "the grave" interchangeably. One sev-2 case is flagged: 9:18.
- Italicised supplied words fill out the Hebrew's terse poetry.
- Musical terms are left obscure, e.g. "Neginoth" and "Muth-labben".

## Most important differences

1. **22:17 (KJV 22:16): "they pierced my hands and my feet"** against the MT's *ka'ari*, "like a lion". The KJV follows the LXX and Syriac (*ka'aru*). This is TEXT 3, the one major textual crux in the book.
2. **8:6: "lower than the angels"** against *me'elohim*, "than God". The KJV follows the LXX, which Heb 2:7 also follows.
3. **100:3: "and not we ourselves"** follows the ketiv *lo'*. The qere *lo* gives "and we are his".
4. **58:2: "O congregation"** against *'elem*. The text is usually emended to *'elim*, "you gods".
5. **82:1 and 29:1: "the mighty"** against the divine council or heavenly beings, *'el* and *bene 'elim*.
6. **19:4: "no speech nor language, where their voice is not heard"** reverses the Hebrew, which says the heavens' voice is unheard.
7. **22:22 and 92:11: "unicorn"** for *re'em*, the wild ox.
8. **4:3: "leasing" (lies), 50:23: "conversation" (way of life) and 59:11: "prevent" (meet)** are archaisms that now mislead.

## Assessment

The KJV Psalter is a faithful translation of the Masoretic Text in most places. About 95% of verses need no comment.

Its distinctive divergences fall into three groups:

- **Septuagint-influenced readings at messianic cruxes:** 22:17, 8:6 and the "Son" in 2:12.
- **Divine-council language softened** into "mighty" or "congregation".
- **Obscure poetic lines** where 17th-century Hebrew lexicography guessed: animals, instruments and hapax words.

The theological shape of the Psalms is intact. Readers should be aware of the textual choice at 22:16 and of the LXX influence that the NT quotations carry back into the Psalter.
