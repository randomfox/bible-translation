# Genesis 1–50: summary

**Coverage:** 50 chapters, 1,533 verses (Masoretic numbering, which matches the KJV).

**Flags:** 158 flags.
- By category: INTERP 47, LEXICAL 43, ARCHAIC 33, MEANING 24, NAME 9, TEXT 2.
- By severity: 132 at sev 1, 25 at sev 2, 1 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Divine names.** YHWH is rendered "the LORD"; *Elohim* is "God"; *El Shaddai* is "God Almighty". These are noted here, not flagged each time.
- **Textual base.** Both versions follow the Masoretic Text, so there are only 2 textual flags.
- **Where the differences come from.** Almost all come from three sources:
  - rare words the 1611 translators guessed at, often by following the Targums or medieval Jewish commentators;
  - archaic English;
  - messianic or Christian readings in disputed verses.

## Most important differences

1. **49:10 (sev 3).** "until Shiloh come". This is the most debated crux in Genesis. The Hebrew can be read as "until the One to Whom it belongs comes" or as "until tribute comes to him". The KJV turns it into a messianic name.
2. **3:15 and 22:18 / 26:4.** In 3:15 the KJV's "it shall bruise thy head" treats the offspring collectively. In 22:18 and 26:4 the reflexive "bless themselves by your offspring" is made passive, "be blessed", which gave the verses their later Pauline reading.
3. **24:43.** "the virgin" for *almah*, which means a young woman. Virginity is stated separately, with *betulah*, in 24:16. Compare Isaiah 7:14.
4. **2:7.** "a living soul" for *nephesh hayyah*. The same phrase is used of animals in chapter 1.
5. **32:28–29.** The name Israel is explained in the KJV as "as a prince hast thou power". The Hebrew means "you have striven".
6. **37:3.** "coat of many colours" for what was probably a long robe with sleeves.
7. **Archaic English.** "replenish" for fill (1:28), "tempt" for test (22:1), "giants" for the Nephilim (6:4), "whales" for sea creatures (1:21).
8. **Guesses at rare words.** "mules" for hot springs (36:24), "digged down a wall" for hamstrung oxen (49:6), "a grove" for a tamarisk (21:33).

## Assessment

The KJV's Genesis is a magnificent and generally faithful rendering of the Hebrew narratives. The translators made almost no textual changes. Where they diverge, it is because 17th-century Hebrew scholarship was uncertain about rare words, or because they let Christian reading shape a handful of ambiguous promise texts (3:15, 22:18, 49:10). One in ten verses carries a flag, mostly minor.

*Caveat: KJV comparisons were checked at selected verses and known cruxes.*
