## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- The KJV gives no speaker labels. Neither do I; the Hebrew has none, and speakers are inferred from gender endings. Those endings are clear in Hebrew but invisible in KJV English ("thee/thou" for both sexes).
- "Fair" for *yafah* means beautiful.
- "Roe/hart" means gazelle and stag.
- The KJV's 1611 chapter headings, absent from the plain text used here, read the Song as allegory: "the mutual love of Christ and his church". They are not part of the translation itself.

## Most important differences

1. **2:7, 3:5, 8:4: "nor awake my love, till he please"** should be "awaken love until it pleases". The Hebrew warns against rousing passion before its time. The KJV's supplied "my" and "he" turn the refrain into a reference to the sleeping beloved.
2. **2:1: "the rose of Sharon"** should be "a crocus of Sharon". The KJV's article and its "rose" created a title, later applied to Christ.
3. **2:5: "flagons"** should be "raisin cakes".
4. **1:9: "a company of horses"** should be "my mare among Pharaoh's chariots".
5. **4:1, 4:3, 6:7: "within thy locks"** should be "behind your veil".
6. **8:6: "most vehement flame"** may be "the flame of Yah", which would be the only possible divine name in the book.
7. **7:10 (KJV 7:9): "lips of those that are asleep"** comes from the MT, against the versions' "lips and teeth".

## Assessment

The KJV renders the Song faithfully and beautifully. No passage is seriously distorted.

The differences that remain are mostly:

- rare words for plants, objects and anatomy that 17th-century lexicons guessed at;
- English that hides the Hebrew's gender distinctions, so the dialogue between the woman and the man is harder to follow.

The book's frank love poetry survives intact under the KJV's restrained diction.
