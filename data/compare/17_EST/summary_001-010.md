# Esther 1–10: summary

**Coverage:** 10 chapters, 167 verses (Hebrew text only). The Greek Additions to Esther are not part of the 66-book canon, and the KJV likewise prints only the Hebrew.

**Flags:** 9 flags.
- By category: INTERP 3, LEXICAL 3, ARCHAIC 2, MEANING 1.
- By severity: 7 at sev 1, 2 at sev 2.

## Most important differences

1. **7:4.** KJV: "although the enemy could not countervail the king's damage". The Hebrew is obscure. Esther most likely means that their mere enslavement would not have been worth troubling the king.
2. **8:10.** KJV: "riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries". The Persian loanwords describe swift royal horses, not camels.

## Assessment

The KJV renders Esther's court narrative faithfully. Like the Hebrew, it never mentions God, and it adds nothing to fill that silence. The two flags at severity 2 concern obscure phrases and Persian terms. Neither affects the story.
