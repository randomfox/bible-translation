# 1 Samuel 1–31: summary

**Coverage:** 31 chapters, 811 verses. The Hebrew numbering differs from the KJV at 20:42–21:1 and 23:29–24:1.

**Flags:** 74 flags.
- By category: INTERP 25, LEXICAL 16, ARCHAIC 12, MEANING 9, TEXT 7, NAME 5.
- By severity: 69 at sev 1, 5 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The Masoretic text of Samuel is the most damaged in the Hebrew Bible. The Septuagint and the Qumran scroll 4QSam(a) often preserve better readings. Both the KJV and this translation follow the Masoretic Text. The TEXT flags mark places where both versions keep a reading that is probably corrupt, and places where the KJV quietly repairs one (13:1, 3:13).

## Most important differences

1. **13:1.** "Saul reigned one year; and when he had reigned two years". The Hebrew is the standard formula giving a king's age and the length of his reign, "Saul was … years old", but the numbers are missing. The KJV hides the gap by inventing a sense.
2. **13:21.** "a file for the mattocks". The word *pim* was unknown in 1611. Inscribed weights found by archaeologists show it is a weight, two-thirds of a shekel, so the verse gives the price the Philistines charged for sharpening.
3. **28:13.** "I saw gods ascending". The medium sees "a divine being", meaning the spirit of Samuel.
4. **3:13.** "made themselves vile". The Masoretic Text reflects a scribal correction made out of reverence. The original reading was "cursed God".

## Assessment

The stories of Samuel, Saul and David come through vividly and faithfully in the KJV. The book's well-known textual damage affects both versions alike. The KJV's distinctive weakness is that it smooths over gaps (13:1) rather than marking them.
