# Hebrews 1–13: summary

**Coverage:** 13 chapters and 303 verses. Same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 28 flags.
- By category: TEXT 14, INTERP 6, LEXICAL 5, MEANING 2, NAME 1.
- By severity: 26 at sev 1, 2 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The TR:
  - completes Old Testament quotations (2:7, 7:21, 12:20),
  - adds phrases from parallel passages (3:6, 8:12, 11:13), and
  - assumes Paul wrote the letter: "me in my bonds" (10:34).
- **"Testament" and "covenant".** Both render the same word, *diatheke*. In chapters 7–10 the KJV alternates between them. Only 9:16–17 is a deliberate wordplay meaning "will"; elsewhere the alternation blurs the argument from Jeremiah 31.
- **"Perfect" and "perfection".** These render *teleioo* and *teleiotes*, which mean maturity or completion (5:14, 6:1, 12:2). The KJV wording has been read in support of doctrines of sinless perfection.

## Most important differences

1. **4:8 (sev 2).** KJV "if Jesus had given them rest". The Greek name is Joshua's, and the author means Joshua. The KJV makes him say the opposite of his argument.
2. **2:16 (sev 2).** KJV "he took not on him the nature of angels". The italic words are supplied by the translators. The Greek says Christ "takes hold of", that is, helps, Abraham's offspring, not angels. The KJV turns a statement about whom Christ rescues into one about the incarnation.
3. **10:23.** KJV "the profession of our faith". The Greek in every edition reads "hope". This is a plain mistranslation.
4. **6:6.** KJV "If they shall fall away". The Greek states that they have fallen away; it is not a hypothesis. The KJV softens one of the NT's sternest warnings.
5. **11:1.** KJV "substance … evidence". This is defensible but now abstract; "assurance … conviction" is more natural.
6. **12:2.** KJV "author and finisher of our faith". The "our" is supplied in italics.
7. **9:11.** KJV "good things to come". The early text reads "that have come".

## Assessment

The KJV's Hebrews is an eloquent and generally accurate rendering of the most polished Greek in the New Testament. Its great passages read faithfully: 1:1–4, 4:12–16, 11 and 12:1–2. The two severity-2 errors (4:8 "Jesus" and 2:16 "the nature of angels") and the plain mistranslation at 10:23 come from the translators, not from the TR. That makes Hebrews unusual among the epistles in this comparison.
