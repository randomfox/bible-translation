# Titus 1–3: summary

**Coverage:** 3 chapters, 46 verses. Same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 5 flags.
- By category: TEXT 2, INTERP 1, ARCHAIC 1, LEXICAL 1.
- By severity: all at sev 1.

## Most important differences

1. **2:13.** "the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ". In the Greek, one article governs both nouns, so the phrase most naturally names Jesus Christ as "our great God and Savior". The KJV's wording allows a reading with two persons. On this point the KJV is less explicit about Christ's deity than the Greek, which is the reverse of 1 Tim 3:16.
2. **2:14.** "a peculiar people". In 1611 "peculiar" meant "his own possession". Today it means "odd".
3. **3:10.** "an heretick". The Greek describes a divisive person, not someone who holds false doctrine.
4. **1:4.** The TR adds "mercy" to the greeting, borrowed from 1–2 Timothy.

## Assessment

Titus is short, practical and accurately rendered. The baptismal passage (3:4–7) reads essentially as in a modern translation. The only theologically notable point is the syntax of 2:13, where the KJV leaves an ambiguity that the Greek does not have.
