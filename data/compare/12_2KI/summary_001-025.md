# 2 Kings 1–25: summary

**Coverage:** 25 chapters, 719 verses. The Hebrew numbering differs from the KJV at 11:21–12:1.

**Flags:** 72 flags.
- By category: LEXICAL 27, INTERP 16, MEANING 9, ARCHAIC 8, NAME 7, TEXT 5.
- By severity: 57 at sev 1, 15 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **"Grove" for Asherah (17:10, 17:16, 18:4, 21:3, 21:7, 23:6, 23:14).** The Hebrew word is a manufactured object: something "made", carved (21:7), or carried out of the temple (23:6). It cannot be a grove of trees.
- **"Sodomites" for *qedeshim* (23:7).** As in 1 Kings, these are male shrine personnel.

## Most important differences

1. **2:23.** "little children" mock Elisha. *Ne'arim* covers youths and young men. The story is troubling either way, but "little children" makes it harsher.
2. **3:27.** "there was great indignation against Israel". The Hebrew word is *qetsef*, which normally means divine wrath. Whose wrath falls on Israel after the Moabite king's sacrifice is deliberately left unclear.
3. **23:29.** "went up against the king of Assyria". The Babylonian Chronicle shows that Pharaoh Neco went to aid Assyria, not to fight it.
4. **22:14.** "in the college". The Hebrew means the Second Quarter, a district of Jerusalem.
5. **19:7.** "I will send a blast upon him". The Hebrew means God will put a spirit, or impulse, in Sennacherib.

## Assessment

The KJV's 2 Kings is accurate in its narrative. Its flags are mainly cult vocabulary (the "grove" appears more often here than in any other book) and a few historical details that later discoveries have clarified (23:29). The Elisha story at 2:23 is one case where the English choice changes a reader's moral response.
