## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- **Hebel**, the book's keyword (38 times), is literally "vapor, breath". The KJV renders it "vanity", which in 1611 meant emptiness or transience. Modern readers often hear it as conceit.
- I render it "vapor" throughout, which keeps the image of something fleeting and ungraspable.
- **"Vexation of spirit"** for *re'ut ruach*, "chasing after wind", recurs seven times. It is flagged once, at 1:14.
- "The Preacher" for *Qohelet*, "the assembler", is kept in both versions.
- LORD does not occur. The book uses only *ha'elohim*, "God".

## Most important differences

1. **1:14 and repeats: "vexation of spirit"** should be "chasing after wind". The KJV turns an image of futile pursuit into inner distress.
2. **3:21: "the spirit of man that goeth upward"** is read by the Masoretes and the KJV as a statement. The syntax and the LXX make it a question: *who knows whether* the human spirit goes up? This difference matters for the book's view of the afterlife.
3. **3:11: "he hath set the world in their heart"** should be "eternity". *'Olam* meant "world" only in later Hebrew.
4. **2:8: "musical instruments, and that of all sorts"** renders *shiddah weshiddot*, most likely "concubines".
5. **10:11: "a babbler is no better"** should be "no advantage to the charmer". *Ba'al hallashon* is the snake-charmer.
6. **8:10: "forgotten"** versus "praised": the wicked were honoured in the city. The difference rests on a one-letter variant.
7. **5:5 (KJV 5:6): "the angel"** is probably the temple messenger or priest.

## Assessment

The KJV renders Ecclesiastes accurately in most verses. Its famous lines, such as 3:1–8, 9:11 and 12:1–7, match the Hebrew closely.

The main distortion is cumulative rather than local. "Vanity" and "vexation of spirit" give the book a moralising, self-reproachful tone. The Hebrew metaphors (vapor, chasing wind) are more existential and less condemning.

The other significant divergences come from:

- the Masoretic pointing at 3:21, which softens the book's scepticism about the afterlife;
- a handful of rare words that were guessed in 1611.
