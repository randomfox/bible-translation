# Romans 1–16: summary

**Coverage:** 16 chapters, 433 verses, the same as the KJV.

**Source of the doxology (16:25–27).** The SBLGNT editor prints 16:24, the grace benediction, and leaves the doxology out of the main text altogether. It appears only in the SBLGNT apparatus. Westcott–Hort, Tregelles and NA28 make the opposite choice: they omit 16:24 and print the doxology as 16:25–27, all three with the same wording. This translation follows the SBLGNT for 16:24 and takes 16:25–27 from those three editions. The build script adds the doxology from the apparatus and labels it with its source.

**Flags:** 34 flags.
- By category: TEXT 18, LEXICAL 10, INTERP 6.
- By severity: 33 at sev 1, 1 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** In Paul the TR's additions are smaller than in the Gospels and Acts. It completes quotations (9:28, 10:15, 13:9), adds balancing clauses (11:6, 14:6) and supplies "Christ" or "gospel" (1:16, 15:29). Only 8:1 changes the argument: the TR imports "who walk not after the flesh" from 8:4, which makes "no condemnation" conditional.
- **Legal vocabulary.** Paul's courtroom terms are sometimes blurred:
  - "remission" for *paresis*, which means passing over (3:25).
  - "freed" for *dedikaiotai*, which means acquitted (6:7).
  - "damnation" or "damned" for *krima* and *katakrino*, judgment and condemnation (13:2, 14:23).
- **"Godhead."** The KJV uses it for two different words, *theiotes* (divine nature, 1:20) and *theotes* (deity, Col 2:9).

## Most important differences

1. **3:25 (sev 2).** "for the remission of sins that are past". Paul says God *passed over* former sins in forbearance. That is why the cross was needed to show his justice. The KJV's "remission" (forgiveness) undoes the logic of the verse.
2. **8:1.** The TR adds a condition to "no condemnation".
3. **5:11.** "atonement" for *katallage*, reconciliation. In 1611 the word meant "at-one-ment"; today readers hear expiation.
4. **3:22.** The KJV's "faith of Jesus Christ" is literal and keeps a genuine ambiguity: faith *in* Christ, or Christ's own faithfulness. Many modern versions settle it in favour of "faith in Christ". On this point the KJV is the more neutral.
5. **16:1.** Phoebe is called "servant", where the same Greek word is rendered "deacons" for men in Phil 1:1 and 1 Tim 3.
6. **14:10.** "judgment seat of Christ" against "of God".
7. **16:25–27.** The doxology appears in different places in the manuscripts: after 14:23, after 15:33, after 16:23, in two places, or not at all. The KJV prints it at the end of the letter, as NA28 does. It renders *chronois aioniois* as "since the world began"; the Greek means "through long ages".
8. **7:6.** The KJV reads "that being dead wherein we were held", which makes the law the one that dies. Paul says *we* died to it.

## Assessment

Romans is the KJV at its strongest. The great passages read almost exactly as in a modern critical translation: 1:16–17, 3:21–26, 5:1–11, chapter 8, 11:33–36 and 12:1–2. Only one flag reaches severity 2. The differences are fine points of Paul's legal and covenant vocabulary, and a handful of TR additions that balance clauses. The 1611 translators handled Paul's dense argument with great care. Some of their key words have since changed meaning: "atonement", "damnation", "reasonable service" and "Godhead". A modern reader therefore meets more distance in vocabulary than in the underlying Greek.
