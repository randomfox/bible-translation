# Daniel 1–12 — summary

- **Coverage:** 12 chapters, 357 verses, in Hebrew/Aramaic numbering. The source is Hebrew in 1:1–2:4a and 8–12, and Aramaic in 2:4b–7:28.
- **Flags:** 20 verses flagged, 20 flags.
  - **By category:** INTERP 9, MEANING 6, LEXICAL 3, ARCHAIC 1, NAME 1.
  - **By severity:** sev 1: 11, sev 2: 8, sev 3: 1.

## Systematic

- **Numbering:** the chapter breaks differ. Heb 3:31–33 = KJV 4:1–3, Heb 4 = KJV 4:4–37, and Heb 6:1 = KJV 5:31.
- **Divine names:** YHWH occurs only in ch. 9; the KJV renders it "LORD". The Aramaic titles "God of heaven", "Most High" and "Ancient of Days" are rendered alike in both versions.
- **Textual base:** the KJV follows the Masoretic Text throughout. It omits the Greek Additions (the Prayer of Azariah, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon), as this translation does.
- **Rendering of anointed-figure passages:** the main pattern is that the KJV reads Christian meanings into Daniel's visions. Wherever a phrase has no article, the KJV capitalises it into a title:
  - "the Son of God" (3:25)
  - "the Son of man" (7:13)
  - "the most Holy" (9:24)
  - "the Messiah the Prince" (9:25)

## Most important differences

1. **9:25 (sev 3)**
   - KJV: "unto the Messiah the Prince shall be seven weeks, and threescore and two weeks".
   - Mine: "until an anointed one, a prince, … seven weeks. Then for sixty-two weeks…"
   - The Masoretic accent divides 7 from 62. Joining them into 69 weeks to "the Messiah" is the basis of the traditional Christian chronology.
2. **9:26**
   - KJV: "Messiah be cut off, but not for himself".
   - Mine: "an anointed one shall be cut off and shall have nothing".
   - *ve'en lo* is not a vicarious "for himself".
3. **3:25**
   - KJV: "like the Son of God".
   - Mine: "like a son of the gods".
   - A pagan king speaks of a divine being, and in 3:28 he calls it "his angel".
4. **7:13**
   - KJV: "like the Son of man".
   - Mine: "like a son of man".
   - The phrase means a human-like figure, contrasted with the beasts.
5. **8:14**
   - KJV: "2300 days … cleansed".
   - Mine: "2,300 evenings and mornings … restored".
   - This may count the evening and morning offerings separately, which would give 1,150 days. The KJV reading underlies historicist date-setting.
6. **2:5, 2:8**
   - KJV: "The thing is gone from me".
   - Mine: "The word from me is firm".
   - *azda* is a Persian word for "certain". The king had not forgotten his dream.
7. **7:9**
   - KJV: "thrones were cast down".
   - Mine: "thrones were placed".
   - The thrones are set up for the heavenly court, not overthrown.
8. **11:37**
   - KJV: "the God of his fathers".
   - Mine: "the gods of his fathers".
   - The king is a Seleucid who neglects his ancestral Greek gods.

## Assessment

Outside the prophetic chapters the KJV of Daniel is close and reliable. The court tales in chs. 1–6 show only lexical issues: the instrument names, "Melzar" (a title read as a name) and "pulse".

The visions are another matter. Daniel has the highest share of sev 2–3 flags of any book so far (9 of 20). They cluster where the KJV reads an indefinite Aramaic or Hebrew phrase as a definite Christian title, or overrides the Masoretic accents (9:25). These are not errors of vocabulary. They are interpretive decisions consistent with the translators' theology, and they still shape popular end-times readings. A reader who wants Daniel in its own historical frame (Antiochus IV, the Maccabean crisis) will find the KJV steering elsewhere at exactly the key verses.

Caveat: the KJV check covered selected verses, not every verse in full.
