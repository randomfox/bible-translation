# 2 Corinthians 1–13: summary

**Coverage:** 13 chapters and 256 SBLGNT verses. The KJV has 257 because it splits SBLGNT 13:12 into two verses (KJV 13:12–13). SBLGNT 13:13 is therefore KJV 13:14. No verses are missing.

**Flags:** 16 flags.
- By category: LEXICAL 7, TEXT 6, INTERP 3.
- By severity: all 16 at sev 1.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The TR differs from the critical text only slightly:
  - "ye" for "we" at 6:16;
  - "my strength" for "power" at 12:9;
  - "all things are become new" at 5:17;
  - added "Christ" at 11:31 and "Amen" at 13:13.
- **Word choice.** The flags cluster in Paul's vivid metaphors, which the KJV flattens:
  - the Roman triumph, in which Paul is a captive (2:14);
  - the huckster selling God's word (2:17);
  - the down payment of the Spirit, the KJV's "earnest" (1:22, 5:5);
  - the arguments torn down in 10:5, which the KJV calls "imaginations";
  - the test that is passed or failed, the KJV's "reprobates" (13:5–7).

## Most important differences

1. **2:14.** The KJV has "causeth us to triumph". Paul pictures himself led as a captive in Christ's victory parade, not as a conqueror.
2. **5:17.** The TR adds "all things", so the KJV reads "all things are become new". The early text says "the new has come", announcing the new creation that has broken in.
3. **11:5 and 12:11.** The KJV has "the very chiefest apostles". The phrase is a sarcastic label, "super-apostles", for Paul's rivals, but the KJV wording suggests he is comparing himself with Peter and the Twelve.
4. **13:5.** The KJV has "reprobates". It makes a call to self-examination sound like a question of eternal damnation.
5. **5:11.** The KJV has "terror of the Lord" where the Greek is the "fear of the Lord" of the wisdom tradition.

## Assessment

2 Corinthians is among the KJV's most accurate books in this comparison. There are no verses missing, no flags at severity 2 or above, and few textual differences. Its famous lines are faithful to the Greek:
- "treasure in earthen vessels" (4:7);
- "we walk by faith, not by sight" (5:7);
- "a new creature" (5:17);
- "my grace is sufficient for thee" (12:9).

Most of the differences come from images that a modern reader no longer recognises.
