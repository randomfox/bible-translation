## Systematic differences (not flagged per verse)

- LORD for YHWH.
- "Groves" for the Asherim.
- "Backsliding" for *meshubah* (apostasy, faithlessness).
- "Lovingkindness/mercy" for *hesed*.
- "Burden" for *massa'*. The 23:33–40 wordplay on *massa'* as "oracle" and "load" is partly lost.
- "Captivity" for *shebut*. The KJV's "turn again the captivity" means "restore the fortunes".
- The KJV follows the longer Masoretic form of Jeremiah throughout. The LXX is about one eighth shorter and orders the oracles against the nations differently. Neither translation follows the LXX arrangement.

## Most important differences

1. **27:1:** the MT and the KJV say "Jehoiakim", but the chapter concerns Zedekiah (vv. 3, 12; 28:1). The Syriac and some manuscripts read "Zedekiah". TEXT 2.
2. **8:8: "the pen of the scribes is in vain"** should be "the lying pen of the scribes has made it into a lie". It is an accusation that the written Torah has been falsified.
3. **10:5: "upright as the palm tree"** should be "like scarecrows in a cucumber field". The ridicule of idols is reversed.
4. **46:25: "the multitude of No"** is Amon of Thebes, the Egyptian god.
5. **17:9: "desperately wicked"** should be "desperately sick" (*'anush*, incurable).
6. **23:33, 23:39:** the versions read "You are the burden" and "I will lift you up". These readings preserve the *massa'* pun that the MT and the KJV lose.
7. **49:1, 3: "their king"** is Milcom, Ammon's god. **51:1: "them that rise up against me"** is Leb-kamai, a code-name for Chaldea.
8. **39:3:** the KJV turns Babylonian titles into personal names. The list includes a Rab-saris whose name, Nabu-sharrussu-ukin, appears on a Babylonian tablet.

## Assessment

Jeremiah's prose (chs. 26–45, 52) is rendered by the KJV with great accuracy. Nearly all its divergences fall in the poetry, in three kinds:

- rare words for plants, animals and objects: tomer, qerets, mitsar, chammanim;
- cipher names and divine names that it translated as ordinary words: Milcom, Amon, Leb-kamai;
- a few Masoretic readings that context or the versions correct: 27:1, 33:2, 46:15.

Theologically central passages, such as the call (1), the temple sermon (7), the letter to the exiles (29) and the new covenant (31:31–34), match the Hebrew closely.

Jeremiah has the lowest flag rate of the major prophets in this project: 3% of verses.
