# 1 Corinthians 1–16: summary

**Coverage:** 16 chapters, 437 verses. Same as the KJV; no verses are missing.

**Flags:** 31 flags.
- By category: TEXT 13, INTERP 8, LEXICAL 8, ARCHAIC 2.
- By severity: 30 at sev 1, 1 at sev 2.

## Systematic

- **"Charity" for *agape*.** Used throughout chapters 8, 13, 14 and 16 (flagged once at 13:1). Today "charity" means almsgiving, which 13:3 shows Paul distinguishes from love.
- **Corinthian slogans.** Paul quotes the Corinthians' own catchphrases and answers them (6:12–13, 7:1, 8:1, 8:4, 10:23). The KJV has no quotation marks, so these read as Paul's own teaching, for example "It is good for a man not to touch a woman".
- **"Unknown tongue".** The italic "unknown" supplied throughout chapter 14 decides what kind of tongues are meant.
- **Textual base.** The TR additions here are small:
  - liturgical words in the Supper narrative ("Take, eat", "broken", 11:24),
  - an ascetic "fasting" (7:5),
  - a softening phrase "and in your spirit" (6:20), and
  - "the Lord" in 15:47.

## Most important differences

1. **11:29 (sev 2).** "eateth and drinketh damnation to himself". *Krima* is judgment, and 11:30–32 explains it as illness and discipline "that we should not be condemned". The TR also adds "unworthily" and "Lord's" here. For centuries this verse has kept anxious believers from communion.
2. **6:9.** "effeminate, abusers of themselves with mankind". The euphemisms hide what the Greek terms refer to; their exact sense is itself debated.
3. **7:36–38.** "his virgin" for the betrothed woman. This has led to readings in which the passage is about a father and his daughter.
4. **13:5.** "not easily provoked": the KJV adds "easily". "Thinketh no evil" means "keeps no record of wrongs".
5. **11:10.** "power on her head" for *exousia* (authority). It is often read as a veil, a sign of the husband's power.
6. **15:55.** "O grave, where is thy victory?" The second line reads "death" in the early text.
7. **16:22.** "Anathema Maranatha" is two expressions run together: "let him be accursed" and the Aramaic prayer "Our Lord, come!".

## Assessment

The great passages read beautifully and accurately: the cross as folly (1:18–25), the body (chapter 12), love (chapter 13) and resurrection (chapter 15). The differences are matters of nuance and of changed English: "charity", "through a glass", "know nothing by myself", "castaway". The one serious misreading is "damnation" at 11:29. Its pastoral effect on generations of readers outweighs its single flag.
