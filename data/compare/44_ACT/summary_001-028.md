# Acts 1–28: summary

**Coverage:** 28 chapters and 1,002 SBLGNT verses. The KJV has 1,007. Four KJV verses have no counterpart in the critical text: 8:37, 15:34, 24:7 and 28:29. KJV 19:41 is part of 19:40 in the SBLGNT numbering.

**Flags:** 54 flags.
- By category: TEXT 30, LEXICAL 9, INTERP 8, MEANING 4, NAME 3.
- By severity: 44 at sev 1, 10 at sev 2, none at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** Acts survives in two early forms: the shorter Alexandrian text (P74, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus) and a "Western" text about 8% longer (Codex Bezae). The TR draws in many Western expansions: whole verses (8:37, 15:34, 24:7, 28:29) and explanatory phrases (10:6, 15:24, 18:21, 21:25, 28:16).
- **One reading the TR found in no Greek manuscript.** At 9:5–6, "it is hard for thee to kick against the pricks. And he trembling and astonished said, Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?" was translated by Erasmus from the Latin Vulgate. No Greek manuscript has it at this point.
- **Recurring terms.** Not flagged per verse: "Holy Ghost"; "hell" for *hades* (flagged at 2:27); "Prince" for *archegos* (flagged at 3:15).
- **Pagan names.** The KJV gives Roman names for Greek gods: Jupiter and Mercurius for Zeus and Hermes (14:12), Diana for Artemis (19:24–35).

## Most important differences

1. **8:37 (sev 2).** The eunuch's confession "I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God". A baptismal formula added to the text. It is absent from every early Greek manuscript.
2. **9:5–6 (sev 2).** Words from 26:14 and 22:10, imported through the Latin. The only TR reading in Acts with no Greek basis at this point.
3. **12:4 (sev 2).** "Easter" for *pascha*. Everywhere else the KJV renders this word "passover", and it is wrong here, since 12:3 dates the arrest to Unleavened Bread.
4. **3:13, 3:26, 4:27, 4:30 (sev 2).** "his Son Jesus", "thy holy child Jesus" for *pais*. In these speeches the word is "servant" and echoes Isaiah's Servant, an early Christology that the KJV hides.
5. **7:45 (sev 2).** "Jesus" for Joshua, so Stephen appears to say Jesus led Israel into Canaan.
6. **17:22 (sev 2).** "ye are too superstitious". Paul opens his Areopagus speech with a courtesy ("very religious"), not an insult.
7. **26:28 (sev 2).** "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian". Agrippa's line is a dry deflection ("In a short time would you persuade me…?"), not a near-conversion.
8. **15:34, 24:7, 28:29 (sev 2).** Western additions that smooth the narrative.

## Assessment

In Acts, the gap between the KJV and the critical text is mainly textual, as in the Gospels, but the cause is different. Here the TR follows a distinct expanded edition of Acts, not harmonisations. The KJV's rendering of Luke's Greek is generally careful, but Acts contains more translation errors with real consequences than any Gospel: "Easter" (12:4), "Jesus" for Joshua (7:45), "too superstitious" (17:22), "Almost thou persuadest me" (26:28), and the loss of the Servant title in Peter's speeches. Several of these have shaped preaching and hymnody for four centuries.
