# Deuteronomy 1–34: summary

**Coverage:** 34 chapters, 959 verses, using Masoretic numbering. Heb 12:32 = KJV 13:1, and Heb 22:30 = KJV 23:1, so the verse numbers in chapters 12–13 and 22–23 differ by one.

**Flags:** 119 flags.
- By category: LEXICAL 39, INTERP 35, MEANING 21, ARCHAIC 19, TEXT 3, NAME 2.
- By severity: 98 at sev 1, 20 at sev 2, 1 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **"Giants" for Rephaim.** The KJV renders *repha'im*, an ancient people, as "giants" (2:11, 2:20, 3:11).
- **"Groves" for *asherim*.** These were cult poles of the goddess Asherah, not groves of trees (7:5, 16:21).
- **"Unicorn" for *re'em*.** The word means the wild ox (33:17).
- **Law terms whose English sense has shifted:**
  - "usury" for any interest on a loan (23:20);
  - "bastard" for *mamzer*, a child of a forbidden union (23:3);
  - "whore" and "sodomite" for persons attached to a shrine (23:18).

## Most important differences

1. **6:4 (sev 3).** The *Shema*. The KJV reads "The LORD our God is one LORD". The verbless Hebrew can also mean "Yahweh is our God, Yahweh alone", which is a statement of exclusive loyalty rather than of divine unity. Both readings are ancient.
2. **5:17.** "Thou shalt not kill" for *ratsach*, which means murder (as in Exod 20:13).
3. **24:1–4.** "then let him write her a bill of divorcement". In the Hebrew, verses 1–3 are one long condition; the only command comes in verse 4, which forbids remarriage to the first husband. The KJV turns the condition into a command to divorce, a misreading that Jesus addresses in Mark 10.
4. **26:5.** "A Syrian ready to perish was my father". The Hebrew means "a wandering Aramean". It refers to Jacob, at the heart of Israel's creed.
5. **18:15.** "a Prophet". The KJV capitalises the word and makes it a messianic title. In context it means the line of true prophets, as opposed to diviners.
6. **15:4.** The KJV's "Save when there shall be no poor" obscures the promise that there will be none, which stands in tension with 15:11.
7. **32:43.** Both versions follow the short Masoretic text. The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Septuagint have a longer form, which Heb 1:6 quotes.

## Assessment

The KJV's Deuteronomy is eloquent and in the main accurate. Its most consequential choices fall in the law code: "kill" for murder, the divorce law turned into a permission, "usury", and the cultic terms. The *Shema*'s "one LORD" is a defensible reading, but it is one of several.
