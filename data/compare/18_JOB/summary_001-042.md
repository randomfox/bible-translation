# Job 1–42: summary

**Coverage:** 42 chapters, 1,070 verses (Hebrew numbering, which matches the KJV except at 40:25–41:26, KJV 41:1–34).

**Flags:** 71 flags.
- By category: LEXICAL 30, INTERP 18, MEANING 15, TEXT 5, ARCHAIC 2, NAME 1.
- By severity: 51 at sev 1, 17 at sev 2, 3 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Difficult Hebrew.** Job's Hebrew is the most difficult in the Bible: there are many hapax legomena (words that occur only once), and the poetry is dense. The KJV often guesses. In about a dozen places later knowledge of Hebrew and of related languages points to a different meaning.
- **Mythological creatures.** Many of the Hebrew Bible's references to the chaos-sea and its monsters are in Job: Leviathan (3:8), *tannin* (7:12), Rahab (9:13), the fleeing serpent (26:13). The KJV usually turns them into ordinary words: "mourning", "whale", "the proud", "crooked serpent".
- **Animals.** The KJV renders some animals with real or mythical creatures that do not fit the Hebrew: "unicorn" for the wild ox (39:9), "dragons" for jackals (30:29), "peacocks" for ostriches (39:13).

## Most important differences

1. **13:15 (sev 3).** KJV: "Though he slay me, yet will I trust in him". The written Hebrew text (*ketiv*) reads "Behold, he will kill me; I have no hope". The KJV follows the reading tradition (*qere*), which turns Job's defiance into a famous confession of trust.
2. **19:25–26 (sev 3).** KJV: "I know that my Redeemer liveth … in my flesh shall I see God". The words "worms", "body" and "day" are supplied in italics. *Mibbesari* can mean "from (outside) my flesh". The KJV and Handel made this a clear text about bodily resurrection. The Hebrew is much less certain.
3. **42:6 (sev 3).** KJV: "I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes". "Myself" is supplied. The Hebrew verb has no object, and *nihamti 'al* can mean "I am comforted concerning" or "I retract". Job's last words are among the most disputed lines in the Bible.
4. **1:6.** KJV: "Satan". The Hebrew *ha-satan*, with the article, is the Accuser, a role in the heavenly court.
5. **28:1–11.** The KJV misses that this passage describes mining. For example, 28:4 is about shafts in which miners dangle on ropes.
6. **3:8, 7:12, 9:13, 26:13.** Leviathan, the sea dragon and Rahab are hidden in the KJV's renderings.

## Assessment

Job is where the KJV and a modern critical translation differ most in the poetic books, and it has three of the OT's severity-3 flags. In each, the 1611 translators chose the reading most consoling to Christian faith: trust (13:15), resurrection (19:25–26) and repentance (42:6). The Hebrew is more ambiguous, and more anguished. The KJV's Job is one of the glories of English literature, but it is in part a more pious book than the Hebrew.
