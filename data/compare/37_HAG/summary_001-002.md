# Haggai 1–2: summary

**Coverage:** 2 chapters, 38 verses. The Hebrew and KJV verse numbering agree.

**Flags:** 1 flag on 1 verse. It is INTERP, severity 2.

## Systematic

- **Divine name:** *YHWH tseva'ot* ("Yahweh of Armies") occurs 14 times in 38 verses. The KJV renders it "the LORD of hosts".
- **Textual base:** both versions follow the Masoretic Text. There is no textual divergence.

## Difference

**2:7**
- **KJV:** "the desire of all nations shall come".
- **Mine:** "the treasures of all nations shall come in".
- **Grammar:** the verb is plural, which fits "treasures" as its subject.
- **Context:** the next verse reads "the silver is mine, and the gold is mine". So the oracle promises the nations' wealth for the rebuilt temple.
- **Effect of the KJV wording:** "the desire of all nations" made this a messianic title. It is the source of the hymn line "Desire of nations, bring". The Vulgate's *desideratus cunctis gentibus* supported that reading.

## Assessment

Haggai is plain prose, and the KJV renders it accurately. Apart from 2:7, it matches the Hebrew in substance throughout. That includes "consider your ways" (1:5, 7), the purity question to the priests (2:11–14), and the signet-ring oracle to Zerubbabel (2:23).

The single flag is a typical KJV pattern. A plural, material phrase is read as a singular, personal, messianic one.
