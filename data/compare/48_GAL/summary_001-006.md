# Galatians 1–6: summary

**Coverage:** 6 chapters, 149 verses. Same as the KJV.

**Flags:** 14 flags.
- By category: TEXT 8, LEXICAL 2, MEANING 2, INTERP 2.
- By severity: all 14 at sev 1.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** The TR differs from the critical text in four kinds of place:
  - It substitutes "Peter" for Paul's Aramaic "Cephas" (1:18, 2:11, 2:14).
  - It adds "Christ" phrases (3:17, 4:7, 6:15).
  - It expands the vice list with "adultery" and "murders" (5:19–21).
  - It imports "that ye should not obey the truth" into 3:1 from 5:7.
- **"Faith of Christ".** The KJV renders *pistis Christou* literally as "faith of Christ" (2:16, 2:20, 3:22). This keeps the ambiguity between faith in Christ and Christ's own faithfulness, which is debated today. Many modern versions decide it one way; the KJV does not.
- **"World" for *aion*.** The KJV has "this present evil world" (1:4) where the Greek means "age". This loses Paul's contrast between the present age and the age to come.

## Most important differences

1. **3:24.** KJV: "the law was our schoolmaster [to bring us] unto Christ". The *paidagogos* was a slave guardian, not a teacher. The phrase "to bring us" is a KJV addition, shown in italics. Paul means "until Christ came", a time limit, which 3:23 and 3:25 confirm.
2. **5:12.** KJV: "cut off". Paul's crude wish is that the circumcisers would castrate themselves.
3. **6:11.** KJV: "how large a letter". Paul means the large handwriting of his own signature, not the length of the letter.
4. **5:22.** KJV: "faith" in the fruit of the Spirit. The word here means faithfulness, which is a virtue, not belief.
5. **1:18, 2:11, 2:14.** "Peter" replaces "Cephas". This partly obscures the fact that Paul consistently uses the Aramaic name.

## Assessment

Galatians is translated accurately, and its key verses match a modern rendering: 2:16, 2:20, 3:13, 3:28, 5:1 and 6:14. The differences are small. They are the TR's smoothing additions and a few renderings that domesticate Paul's sharp images, namely the slave-guardian, the castration jibe and the large handwriting. On the central doctrine of justification by faith, the two versions agree.
