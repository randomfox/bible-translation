# Luke 1–24: summary

**Coverage:** 24 chapters and 1,149 SBLGNT verses. The KJV has 1,151. Two KJV verses have no counterpart in the critical text: 17:36 and 23:17.

**Flags:** 74 flags.
- By category: TEXT 56, INTERP 14, LEXICAL 2, ARCHAIC 1, NAME 1.
- By severity: 64 at sev 1, 10 at sev 2, none at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Textual base.** As in Matthew and Mark, most differences come from the Textus Receptus behind the KJV. In Luke the TR:
  - harmonises with Matthew and Mark (11:2–4, 19:45, 20:23, 22:64, 23:38),
  - imports whole verses (17:36 from Mt 24:40, 23:17 from Mk 15:6), and
  - adds liturgical endings ("Amen" at 24:53).
- **Passages SBLGNT brackets but the KJV prints without mark:**
  - 22:43–44, the angel and the sweat like drops of blood (double brackets).
  - 23:34a, "Father, forgive them" (double brackets).
  - 22:19b–20 and 24:40, 24:51b, 24:52 (single brackets, the "Western non-interpolations"). I translated these and marked them; most scholars now accept them.
- **Recurring terms.** Not flagged per verse: "Holy Ghost", "devils" for *daimonia*, "hell" for *geenna*. "Hell" for *hades* was flagged once, at 16:23.

## Most important differences

1. **11:2–4, the Lord's Prayer (sev 2).** The KJV gives Matthew's longer form ("Our Father which art in heaven … Thy will be done … deliver us from evil"). Luke's own text is short: "Father, may your name be kept holy. Your kingdom come."
2. **2:14 (sev 2).** KJV "good will toward men" against "peace among those with whom he is pleased". One letter (*eudokia/eudokias*) changes the angels' message from general goodwill to God's favour.
3. **22:43–44 and 23:34 (sev 2).** The bloody sweat and the prayer for the executioners are absent from the earliest papyrus (P75) and Vaticanus. The KJV presents both as plain text.
4. **9:55 (sev 2).** The KJV adds "Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them." This is a later expansion of "he turned and rebuked them".
5. **2:33 (sev 2).** KJV "Joseph and his mother" replaces "his father and his mother", a scribal change to avoid calling Joseph Jesus' father.
6. **17:21 (INTERP 2).** KJV "the kingdom of God is within you" (spoken to Pharisees) against "in your midst". The KJV reading underlies a long tradition of the kingdom as an inner state.
7. **16:23 (INTERP 2).** KJV "in hell" for *hades*, the realm of the dead, in the parable of Lazarus and the rich man.
8. **17:36 and 23:17.** Whole verses absent from the earliest manuscripts.

## Assessment

Luke's KJV is a faithful translation of the Greek it had. Only 18 of the 73 flags concern translation rather than the underlying text, and only two of those (17:21, 16:23) carry real interpretive weight. The textual picture is different from Mark's: Luke's major problems are not additions at the end but famous passages in the middle of the passion narrative (22:43–44, 23:34) whose status is genuinely uncertain, and a Lord's Prayer that the TR has filled out from Matthew. A KJV reader meets a Luke harmonised towards Matthew, with Luke's shorter, sharper forms smoothed away.

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses.*
