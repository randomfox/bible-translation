# Matthew 1–28: summary

**Coverage:** 28 chapters, 1,068 verses in the SBLGNT. The KJV has 1,071 verses. 17:21, 18:11 and 23:14 have no counterpart in the critical text.

**Flags:** 66 flags on 64 verses.
- By category: TEXT 44, INTERP 11, LEXICAL 4, MEANING 4, ARCHAIC 2, NAME 1.
- By severity: sev 1 has 56 flags, sev 2 has 10, sev 3 has none.

## Systematic differences

- **Textual base.** Most divergences come from this. The KJV translates the Textus Receptus (Scrivener 1894). I translated the SBL Greek New Testament, which follows the earliest manuscripts (P64, Sinaiticus, Vaticanus). In Matthew the TR repeatedly:
  - harmonises to Mark and Luke (5:44, 9:13, 19:17, 20:22, 27:35)
  - adds liturgical endings (6:13 doxology, 28:20 "Amen")
  - imports whole verses (17:21, 18:11, 23:14)
- **Terms the KJV renders consistently and that this translation renders differently.** These are noted here and not flagged each time.
  - "Holy Ghost" for *pneuma hagion*.
  - "hell" for both *geenna* and *hades*. Flagged once each, at 5:22 and 16:18.
  - "devils" for *daimonia*.
  - "the end of the world" for *synteleia tou aionos*, literally "end of the age". Flagged at 13:39, 24:3 and 28:20.
  - "testament" for *diatheke*.
- **Divine titles.** The two versions agree: Christ, Son of Man, Son of God, kingdom of heaven.

## Most important differences

1. **6:13.** The KJV adds the doxology "For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever." It is a liturgical addition, absent from the early manuscripts.
2. **24:36.** The KJV lacks "nor the Son". The critical text includes it, so Jesus disclaims knowledge of the day.
3. **27:16–17.** "Jesus Barabbas". The TR drops the name Jesus, which loses the irony of the choice between two men called Jesus.
4. **19:17.** The KJV has "Why callest thou me good?", harmonised to Mark. Matthew's own wording is "Why do you ask me about what is good?"
5. **17:21, 18:11, 23:14.** These whole verses are imported from Mark and Luke and are absent from the earliest witnesses.
6. **5:22.** The KJV has "angry … without a cause". The softening phrase *eike* is a later addition.
7. **1:25.** The KJV has "her firstborn son", taken over from Luke 2:7.
8. **27:35.** The KJV adds a fulfilment citation of Psalm 22 taken from John.

## Assessment

As a translation, the KJV of Matthew is excellent. Only 21 of the 66 flags concern how it renders the Greek, and none of those is severe. The meaning differences are few and small: "strain at a gnat", "the end of the world", "lunatick". The substantive gap is textual. The KJV's Greek base carries about 40 accretions that scribes added over centuries, mostly harmonisations with the other Gospels. These flatten Matthew's distinctive wording and add three verses he did not write. A reader of the KJV hears a smoothed, harmonised Matthew. On doctrine the content is essentially the same, but the 24:36 omission and the 6:13 doxology are the most consequential textual points.

*Caveat: TR differences were checked at known variant sites and selected verses, not word by word.*
