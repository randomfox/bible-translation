# 2 Samuel 1–24: summary

**Coverage:** 24 chapters, 695 verses. The Hebrew numbering differs from the KJV at 18:33–19:1.

**Flags:** 74 flags.
- By category: INTERP 27, LEXICAL 21, MEANING 9, TEXT 7, ARCHAIC 7, NAME 3.
- By severity: 60 at sev 1, 13 at sev 2, 1 at sev 3.

## Systematic

- **Softening.** Several KJV renderings smooth over things the Hebrew states plainly, apparently for theological or moral reasons:
  - David's sons "were priests". The KJV has "chief rulers" (8:18).
  - Ira was "a priest to David". The KJV has "chief ruler" (20:26).
  - Michal "bore" five sons. The KJV has "brought up", because 6:23 says she was childless (21:8).
  - The Ammonites were "set to labor with saws". The KJV has "put them under saws", making it torture (12:31).

## Most important differences

1. **21:19 (sev 3).** The Hebrew says Elhanan killed Goliath. The KJV inserts "the brother of" in italics, taking it from 1 Chr 20:5, to harmonise with 1 Samuel 17. This is the clearest case in the Old Testament of the KJV altering the sense of the Hebrew to remove a contradiction.
2. **8:18, 20:26.** "chief rulers" where the Hebrew word is *kohanim*, priests.
3. **12:14.** "given great occasion to the enemies of the LORD". "The enemies of" was added by scribes out of reverence. The underlying sense is "you have utterly scorned Yahweh".
4. **14:14.** "neither doth God respect any person". The Hebrew means "God does not take away life". It is the wise woman's plea for mercy.
5. **1:18.** "teach the children of Judah the use of the bow". "Bow" is probably the title of David's lament, not an order for archery training.
6. **3:29.** "one that leaneth on a staff". The word means a spindle, a woman's tool, so the curse wishes effeminacy on Joab's house.

## Assessment

2 Samuel is the book where the KJV most often softens or harmonises the Hebrew. The cases are David's priestly sons, Michal's children, Elhanan and Goliath, and the fate of the Ammonites. These choices protected the reputations of David and of the Scriptures. In doing so they obscured the frankness of one of the Bible's most candid histories.
