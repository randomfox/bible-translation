18:1	David mustered the people who were with him and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.
18:2	David sent out the troops, a third under the command of Joab, a third under Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and a third under Ittai the Gittite. And the king said to the troops, "I myself will certainly march out with you too."
18:3	But the troops said, "You must not march out. For if we flee, they will not care about us; even if half of us die, they will not care about us. But you are worth ten thousand of us. So it is better that you support us from the city."
18:4	The king said to them, "Whatever seems best to you I will do." So the king stood beside the gate while all the troops marched out by hundreds and by thousands.
18:5	The king commanded Joab, Abishai, and Ittai, "Deal gently for my sake with the young man Absalom." And all the troops heard when the king gave all the commanders orders about Absalom.
18:6	So the troops went out into the field against Israel, and the battle took place in the forest of Ephraim.
18:7	The army of Israel was defeated there by David's servants, and the slaughter there that day was great: twenty thousand men.
18:8	The battle spread out over the face of the whole countryside, and the forest devoured more of the people that day than the sword devoured.
18:9	Absalom happened to meet David's servants. Absalom was riding on his mule, and the mule went under the thick branches of a great oak; his head caught fast in the oak, and he was left hanging between heaven and earth while the mule that was under him went on.
18:10	A certain man saw it and told Joab, "I just saw Absalom hanging in an oak."
18:11	Joab said to the man who told him, "You saw him! Then why did you not strike him to the ground there? I would have had to give you ten pieces of silver and a belt."
18:12	The man said to Joab, "Even if I felt the weight of a thousand pieces of silver in my hands, I would not raise my hand against the king's son, for in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, 'Protect, whoever you are, the young man Absalom.'
18:13	Or if I had acted treacherously at the risk of my own life, and nothing is hidden from the king, you yourself would have stood aloof."
18:14	Joab said, "I will not waste time like this with you." He took three spears in his hand and thrust them into Absalom's heart while he was still alive in the middle of the oak.
18:15	Then ten young men, Joab's armor-bearers, surrounded Absalom and struck him and killed him.
18:16	Then Joab blew the ram's horn, and the troops came back from pursuing Israel, for Joab held the troops back.
18:17	They took Absalom and threw him into a great pit in the forest and raised over him a very great heap of stones. And all Israel fled, each to his own tent.
18:18	Now in his lifetime Absalom had taken and set up for himself the pillar that is in the King's Valley, for he said, "I have no son to keep my name in remembrance." He called the pillar after his own name, and it is called Absalom's Monument to this day.
18:19	Then Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Let me run and carry the good news to the king that Yahweh has vindicated him by delivering him from the hand of his enemies."
18:20	But Joab said to him, "You are not to be the bearer of news today. You may carry news another day, but today you shall not carry news, because the king's son is dead."
18:21	Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, tell the king what you have seen." The Cushite bowed down to Joab and ran.
18:22	Ahimaaz son of Zadok said once more to Joab, "Come what may, let me also run after the Cushite." Joab said, "Why should you run, my son, when you will have no reward for the news?"
18:23	"Come what may, I will run." So he said to him, "Run." Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the plain and outran the Cushite.
18:24	David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, and he lifted up his eyes and looked, and there was a man running alone.
18:25	The watchman called out and told the king. The king said, "If he is alone, there is news in his mouth." And he kept coming closer.
18:26	Then the watchman saw another man running, and the watchman called to the gatekeeper and said, "There is another man running alone." The king said, "He too is bringing news."
18:27	The watchman said, "I think the running of the first is like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok." The king said, "He is a good man and comes with good news."
18:28	Ahimaaz called out and said to the king, "All is well!" He bowed down before the king with his face to the ground and said, "Blessed be Yahweh your God, Who has delivered up the men who raised their hand against my lord the king."
18:29	The king said, "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" Ahimaaz said, "When Joab sent the king's servant, your servant, I saw a great commotion, but I do not know what it was."
18:30	The king said, "Turn aside and stand here." So he turned aside and stood still.
18:31	Then the Cushite came, and the Cushite said, "Good news for my lord the king! For Yahweh has vindicated you today by delivering you from the hand of all who rose up against you."
18:32	The king said to the Cushite, "Is it well with the young man Absalom?" The Cushite said, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise up against you to do you harm, be like that young man."
