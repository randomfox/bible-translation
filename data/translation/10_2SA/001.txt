1:1	After the death of Saul, when David had returned from striking down the Amalekites, David stayed two days in Ziklag.
1:2	On the third day a man came from Saul's camp with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and bowed down.
1:3	David said to him, "Where have you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."
1:4	David said to him, "How did things go? Please tell me." He said, "The troops fled from the battle, and many of the troops have fallen and died; Saul and his son Jonathan are dead too."
1:5	David said to the young man who was reporting to him, "How do you know that Saul and his son Jonathan are dead?"
1:6	The young man who was reporting to him said, "I happened by chance to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul leaning on his spear, and the chariots and the horsemen were closing in on him.
1:7	He turned around, saw me, and called to me, and I said, 'Here I am.'
1:8	He said to me, 'Who are you?' I said to him, 'I am an Amalekite.'
1:9	He said to me, 'Please stand over me and kill me, for agony has seized me, though my life is still wholly in me.'
1:10	So I stood over him and killed him, because I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. Then I took the crown that was on his head and the band that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."
1:11	Then David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and so did all the men who were with him.
1:12	They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and for his son Jonathan, and for the people of Yahweh and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.
1:13	David said to the young man who had reported to him, "Where are you from?" He said, "I am the son of a resident alien, an Amalekite."
1:14	David said to him, "How is it that you were not afraid to put out your hand to destroy Yahweh's anointed?"
1:15	Then David called one of the young men and said, "Come here and strike him down." So he struck him, and he died.
1:16	David said to him, "Your blood be on your own head, for your own mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I killed Yahweh's anointed.'"
1:17	David chanted this lament over Saul and his son Jonathan,
1:18	and he said it should be taught to the people of Judah; it is called "The Bow," and it is written in the Book of Jashar:
1:19	"Your glory, O Israel, lies slain on your heights! How the mighty have fallen!
1:20	Do not tell it in Gath, do not proclaim it in the streets of Ashkelon, or the daughters of the Philistines will rejoice, or the daughters of the uncircumcised will exult.
1:21	Mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew or rain on you, nor fields that yield offerings, for there the shield of the mighty was defiled, the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil.
1:22	From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the bow of Jonathan did not turn back, and the sword of Saul did not return empty.
1:23	Saul and Jonathan, beloved and delightful in their lives, and in their death they were not parted. They were swifter than eagles, they were stronger than lions.
1:24	Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul, who clothed you in scarlet with finery, who put gold ornaments on your garments.
1:25	How the mighty have fallen in the midst of battle! Jonathan lies slain on your heights.
1:26	I grieve for you, my brother Jonathan; you were very dear to me. Your love for me was more wonderful than the love of women.
1:27	How the mighty have fallen, and the weapons of war have perished!"
