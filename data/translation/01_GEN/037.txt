37:1	Jacob settled in the land where his father had sojourned, in the land of Canaan.
37:2	These are the generations of Jacob. Joseph, being seventeen years old, was tending the flock with his brothers; he was a young helper with the sons of Bilhah and the sons of Zilpah, his father's wives. And Joseph brought a bad report about them to their father.
37:3	Now Israel loved Joseph more than all his sons, because he was the son of his old age; and he made him a long robe with sleeves.
37:4	When his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him and could not speak to him peaceably.
37:5	Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers they hated him even more.
37:6	He said to them, "Please listen to this dream that I have dreamed.
37:7	There we were, binding sheaves in the middle of the field, and suddenly my sheaf rose and stood upright, and your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to my sheaf."
37:8	His brothers said to him, "Are you indeed going to reign over us? Are you really going to rule us?" So they hated him even more for his dreams and for his words.
37:9	Then he dreamed yet another dream and recounted it to his brothers. He said, "Look, I have dreamed another dream: the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me."
37:10	When he recounted it to his father and his brothers, his father rebuked him and said to him, "What is this dream that you have dreamed? Are we really to come, I and your mother and your brothers, to bow down to the ground before you?"
37:11	His brothers were jealous of him, but his father kept the matter in mind.
37:12	His brothers went to pasture their father's flock at Shechem.
37:13	Israel said to Joseph, "Are not your brothers pasturing the flock at Shechem? Come, I will send you to them." He said to him, "Here I am."
37:14	So he said to him, "Go now, see how your brothers are doing and how the flock is doing, and bring me back word." So he sent him from the Valley of Hebron, and he came to Shechem.
37:15	A man found him wandering in the countryside, and the man asked him, "What are you looking for?"
37:16	He said, "I am looking for my brothers. Please tell me where they are pasturing the flock."
37:17	The man said, "They have moved on from here, for I heard them say, 'Let us go to Dothan.'" So Joseph went after his brothers and found them at Dothan.
37:18	They saw him from a distance, and before he came near them they plotted against him to kill him.
37:19	They said to one another, "Here comes this dreamer.
37:20	Now come, let us kill him and throw him into one of the pits; then we will say that a wild animal devoured him, and we will see what becomes of his dreams."
37:21	But when Reuben heard it, he rescued him out of their hands, saying, "Let us not take his life."
37:22	Reuben said to them, "Shed no blood. Throw him into this pit here in the wilderness, but do not lay a hand on him" — so that he might rescue him out of their hands and bring him back to his father.
37:23	So when Joseph came to his brothers, they stripped Joseph of his robe, the long robe with sleeves that he had on,
37:24	and they took him and threw him into the pit. The pit was empty; there was no water in it.
37:25	Then they sat down to eat a meal. Looking up, they saw a caravan of Ishmaelites coming from Gilead, their camels carrying gum, balm, and resin, on their way to take it down to Egypt.
37:26	Judah said to his brothers, "What profit is there if we kill our brother and cover up his blood?
37:27	Come, let us sell him to the Ishmaelites, and let our hand not be upon him, for he is our brother, our own flesh." And his brothers agreed.
37:28	Then Midianite traders passed by, and they drew Joseph up and lifted him out of the pit, and sold Joseph to the Ishmaelites for twenty pieces of silver; and they brought Joseph to Egypt.
37:29	When Reuben returned to the pit and saw that Joseph was not in the pit, he tore his clothes.
37:30	He went back to his brothers and said, "The boy is gone! And I — where can I go?"
37:31	Then they took Joseph's robe, slaughtered a male goat, and dipped the robe in the blood.
37:32	They sent the long robe with sleeves and had it brought to their father, saying, "We found this. Please examine it: is it your son's robe or not?"
37:33	He recognized it and said, "It is my son's robe! A wild animal has devoured him; Joseph has surely been torn to pieces."
37:34	Then Jacob tore his garments, put sackcloth on his waist, and mourned for his son many days.
37:35	All his sons and all his daughters rose up to comfort him, but he refused to be comforted and said, "No, I will go down to my son in Sheol mourning." Thus his father wept for him.
37:36	Meanwhile the Medanites sold him in Egypt to Potiphar, an official of Pharaoh, the captain of the guard.
